The Problem

The question "prove it" is no longer hypothetical.

AI systems are taking consequential actions. Hiring decisions. Credit determinations. Insurance underwriting. Compliance workflows.

When regulators, auditors, courts, or counterparties demand proof that an AI did what it claimed to do, organizations face a structural gap. Today's evidence lives in vendor databases, self-attested logs, and governance platform dashboards. None of it is independently verifiable.

That gap is becoming a liability:

  • EU AI Act enforcement begins August 2026 (Article 12 requires automatically generated logs).
  • NYC Local Law 144 already requires bias audits for automated employment decisions.
  • CFPB actively scrutinizes algorithmic lending under fair-lending statutes (ECOA, FCRA).
  • State insurance regulators examine algorithmic underwriting and claims handling.
  • EU Digital Services Act imposes transparency requirements on content moderation systems.

Core Directives

Constitutional Principles

NYRV operates under binding architectural constraints. These are not policies — they are code.

Directive 01

Observe Only

NYRV observes and records. It never decides, never acts, never judges. Recognition without authority.

Directive 02

Append Only

Events can only be added, never modified or deleted. The ledger is permanent. The record is immutable.

Directive 03

No Content Storage

NYRV sees metadata, never sensitive data. The record shows that something happened. NYRV never sees what it contained.

Directive 04

Operator Independence

NYRV maintains no financial or operational relationship with any party whose conduct NYRV observes. Multi-party witness attestation means no single entity, including NYRV, controls the evidence.

Directive 05

Capability Ceiling

NYRV has hard limits. It cannot make autonomous decisions. It cannot execute actions. Decision authority remains with the operators NYRV serves.

Directive 06

Verifiable by Construction

Every claim NYRV makes about an observed AI system is cryptographically verifiable by any third party. The architecture produces evidence the customer does not have to trust NYRV to verify.

Architecture

Architectural Commitments

The architecture produces the trust property. Each commitment is enforced in code and reviewable in the open repository.

Hash-Chained Integrity

Each event includes the hash of the prior event. Any modification breaks the chain. Mathematically provable integrity.

Durable Ledger

PostgreSQL-backed persistence. Append-only schema, enforced in code and at the database level. No update path. No delete path.

Synchronous Evidence

Evidence artifacts returned in the same call as the observation. The recording is confirmed before the operating system proceeds.

Evidence Artifacts

Structured proof returned with every observation. Includes timestamp, hash, chain position, and verification path.

Multi-Party Witness Attestation

Independent institutional witnesses co-sign log checkpoints. No single entity — including NYRV — can rewrite history. Operator independence by architecture, not by promise.

Multi-Anchor External Verification

Checkpoints anchor to public infrastructure through an N-of-M strategy — OpenTimestamps to Bitcoin, RFC 3161 timestamp authorities, OriginStamp to Ethereum. No single anchor failure compromises the record.

Standards Alignment

Standing on open standards.

NYRV operates VeritasChain Protocol (VCP) v1.1 conformant infrastructure. VCP is published by the VeritasChain Standards Organization, a vendor-neutral non-profit advancing the protocol through the IETF SCITT working group.

The discipline of operating against an open standard — rather than inventing a proprietary architecture — means NYRV's customers do not have to take NYRV's word for the architecture's correctness. The specification is public, reviewable, and subject to independent technical scrutiny. NYRV's value is the operated infrastructure that implements it.

NYRV is contributing an Agent Action Profile (AAP) to VeritasChain's domain profile series — a forthcoming specification extending VAP to the AI agent verification domain, alongside the published profiles for algorithmic trading, content provenance, and public administration.

NYRV's architectural commitment to VCP v1.1 conformance is documented in ADR-0002, available in the repository. The document specifies the conformance commitment and the multi-party institutional witness extension operated as a service layer above the protocol.

Integration

How It Works

One call. Synchronous proof. The architecture is simple because the principle is simple.

  1. 1

    Observe

    The operating system sends an event to NYRV when an action occurs.

  2. 2

    Record

    NYRV hashes, chains, and persists the event to the durable ledger.

  3. 3

    Return

    An evidence artifact is returned synchronously with proof of recording.

  4. 4

    Witness

    Chain checkpoints are co-signed by independent institutional witnesses and anchored to public infrastructure. Anyone can verify the log's integrity against multiple parties NYRV does not control.
